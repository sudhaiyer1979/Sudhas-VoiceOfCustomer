What Marketing Should Do Next

Top prioritized recommendations, based on real customer evidence.

Priority 1 ADD

Put Friendship and Alliances at the Center of Positioning

What customers are telling us

Players repeatedly describe playing with friends and forming alliances as the most meaningful part of the experience, often citing it as what makes the grind worthwhile.

What current marketing communicates

Cooperation is mentioned only once, in passing, as part of a single feature-list claim about building a settlement together.

The gap

One of the most emotionally resonant, frequently volunteered positives in reviews is almost absent from the game's core positioning.

Recommended action

ADD friendship, alliances, and shared player stories as a stronger part of Rust's core positioning, not just a footnote in a feature list.

Suggested messaging direction (not final copy)

“Survive together, form alliances, and create stories that last beyond the wipe.”

Backed by 3 piece(s) of real evidence
Priority 2 REFRAME

Turn Offline Raiding Into a Named Feature, Not a Footnote

What customers are telling us

Losing a base to raiders while offline is one of the most vividly described, emotionally intense moments in reviews -- both a source of frustration and a badge of honor.

What current marketing communicates

Raiding is mentioned only as a two-word feature label, 'Base Building and Raiding,' with no acknowledgment of the offline-vulnerability tension that makes it memorable.

The gap

The specific, high-stakes drama of offline raiding, which players describe in vivid detail, isn't reflected in how the feature is marketed.

Recommended action

REFRAME the 'Raiding' feature claim to explicitly name the offline-vulnerability stakes, since that's the part players actually talk about.

Suggested messaging direction (not final copy)

“You can log off. Your base can't.”

Backed by 3 piece(s) of real evidence
Priority 3 ADD

Lead With Crafting Depth and the Fresh-Wipe Feeling

What customers are telling us

Players consistently praise the depth of the crafting and building systems and the excitement of a fresh start after each wipe.

What current marketing communicates

Crafting and building are described only as a plain feature list (tools, armour, weapons; electrical, farming, and automation systems) without conveying the satisfaction players report.

The gap

A strongly and consistently praised feature is presented as a spec sheet rather than an emotional selling point.

Recommended action

ADD language that conveys the satisfaction and sense of possibility players describe, not just a list of what can be crafted.

Suggested messaging direction (not final copy)

“Every wipe is a blank page. What you build on it is up to you.”

Backed by 4 piece(s) of real evidence
Priority 4 ADD

Address Anti-Cheat and Ban Fairness Head-On

What customers are telling us

A recurring, emotionally charged complaint is unfair or unexplained permanent bans, alongside frustration that cheaters remain widespread.

What current marketing communicates

Marketing never mentions anti-cheat, moderation, or fair play at all.

The gap

A significant trust concern for both new and existing players goes completely unaddressed in current messaging.

Recommended action

ADD clear, visible communication about how anti-cheat enforcement and the ban appeals process work.

Suggested messaging direction (not final copy)

“Fair play, enforced -- and a real appeals process when something goes wrong.”

Backed by 3 piece(s) of real evidence
Priority 5 ADD

Set Expectations About the Learning Curve Before Purchase

What customers are telling us

New players often feel blindsided by the steep learning curve and slow start, sometimes leading to early refund attempts, while players who push through describe strong long-term enjoyment.

What current marketing communicates

Marketing describes the harsh starting scenario (naked, armed with a rock) but doesn't warn players that overcoming the learning curve itself takes real time.

The gap

There's a mismatch between the fast, exciting tone of current marketing and the slower, harder onboarding reality new players actually experience.

Recommended action

ADD upfront, honest framing that the first few hours are intentionally hard, so new players arrive with the right expectations instead of bouncing off early.

Suggested messaging direction (not final copy)

“Rust rewards patience. The first few hours are the hardest -- and worth pushing through.”

Backed by 3 piece(s) of real evidence
